Ethical Dilemma Analysis

Insider Misuse of Administrative Privileges (The 2020 Twitter Security Incident) [cite: 115, 118]

The 2020 Twitter security incident stands out as a classic ethical dilemma regarding administrative trust[cite: 118]. During this breach, external threat actors utilized social engineering—specifically targeting human vulnerabilities rather than network vulnerabilities—to compromise internal employees who held high-level administrative credentials[cite: 119]. With access to these internal administration panels, the attackers gained complete control over verified, high-profile accounts (including business leaders and public figures) to spread a fraudulent cryptocurrency scam[cite: 120].

This scenario represents a serious violation of ethical rules and administrative security[cite: 116]. System administrators are granted "the keys to the castle" and hold an implicit covenant to use their elevated privileges responsibly to protect user privacy and system integrity[cite: 117]. When employees failed to recognize social engineering tactics, they unintentionally exposed administrative channels[cite: 119].

Twitter’s initial response included locking down the affected accounts, deleting the scam posts, conducting a forensic investigation, and cooperating with global law enforcement[cite: 124]. To mitigate future insider risks, they restricted administrative access and retrained employees on security awareness[cite: 125]. This case demonstrates that technical tools alone cannot protect a network without ethical stewardship and strong training[cite: 127, 128]. This reinforces my desire to be a security professional whom employers and users can trust completely with sensitive administrative control[cite: 129].

Alternative Ethical Solution

Essential Ethical Principles

  • Integrity: Security professionals must act honestly and responsibly when handling privileged access.
  • Responsibility: Organizations should ensure employees receive ongoing security awareness training and understand how to respond to suspicious requests.
  • Privacy: User information and accounts should always be protected from unauthorized access.
  • Accountability: Employees and organizations should be accountable for following security policies and reporting suspicious activity immediately.

Alternative Approach

Instead of relying primarily on employee judgment, Twitter could have strengthened its security by implementing stricter administrative access controls, mandatory multi-factor authentication for privileged accounts, role-based access permissions, and additional approval requirements before high-risk administrative actions were performed. Regular phishing simulations and security awareness training would also help employees recognize social engineering attacks before sensitive information is exposed.

Why This Approach Is Better

This approach reduces the likelihood that a single employee mistake could compromise critical systems. By combining technical safeguards with employee training, organizations create multiple layers of protection. This not only protects users' personal information but also preserves public trust in the organization. As a future cybersecurity professional, I believe ethical responsibility involves proactively preventing security incidents rather than simply responding after they occur.

Personal Reflection

This case reinforced the importance of integrity, accountability, and service in cybersecurity. My faith teaches me that trust is earned through honest actions and responsible stewardship. As I continue my cybersecurity education, I want to develop both the technical knowledge and ethical character needed to protect others' information and make decisions that reflect my values. I believe combining strong security practices with ethical leadership creates safer systems and stronger organizations.
