Final Reflection: Professional and Spiritual Growth

Looking Back at My Original Goals

When I began developing my Professional and Spiritual Growth Portfolio, my main goal was to better understand how my education and future career in cybersecurity could connect with my faith and personal values. I wanted to develop the technical skills necessary to become a successful cybersecurity professional, but I also wanted to understand what kind of person and professional I wanted to become.

Throughout this course, I have learned that these goals do not need to be separated. My spiritual values can influence the way I approach my education, my career, my relationships, and the responsibilities that come with working in cybersecurity.

My original goals included becoming more knowledgeable and capable in cybersecurity while developing qualities such as integrity, service, and a commitment to continuous learning. As I worked through my portfolio, these goals became more specific. I began to see cybersecurity not only as a technical career but also as an opportunity to protect and serve other people.

How My Goals Have Evolved

One of the biggest changes in my thinking is that I now see professional success as more than technical ability. Technical skills are extremely important in cybersecurity, but they must be combined with ethical judgment and personal integrity.

My analysis of the 2020 Twitter security incident reinforced this idea. The incident demonstrated how access to powerful administrative systems can create serious consequences when security awareness, trust, and ethical responsibility are not strong enough.

This helped me recognize that I want to become a cybersecurity professional who can be trusted with sensitive information and administrative privileges. I do not want my technical knowledge to simply give me the ability to access systems. I want that knowledge to come with the judgment and integrity necessary to use it responsibly.

My goals have also expanded spiritually. I originally thought about spiritual growth and professional growth as two areas that I needed to develop. I now understand them as parts of the same life. My faith in Jesus Christ influences the standards I set for myself as a student and future professional.

What I Learned Through My Projects

The projects included in this portfolio helped me recognize how much my technical knowledge has developed. Creating a Command and Control virtual lab required me to study attack infrastructure, communication channels, and evasion techniques. Designing a simulated enterprise network required teamwork, network architecture, VLAN segmentation, and gateway redundancy. My vulnerability auditing and compliance work introduced me to STIG and SCAP tools and required me to identify security weaknesses and develop appropriate policies.

These experiences helped me understand that cybersecurity requires both technical knowledge and careful decision-making. A cybersecurity professional has to understand how systems work, recognize vulnerabilities, communicate with others, and make decisions that protect people and organizations.

They also helped me connect my technical education to my spiritual principles. My C2 lab demonstrated the importance of proactively understanding threats rather than waiting until a system is compromised. My network project demonstrated the importance of cooperation and reducing single points of failure. My vulnerability assessment demonstrated the value of examining weaknesses honestly so they can be corrected.

These lessons apply beyond technology because they also encourage me to examine my own weaknesses, seek improvement, and rely on the Lord for guidance.

Faith, Integrity, and Service

The spiritual principles I selected for this portfolio—faith, integrity, and continuous learning—have become a framework for how I want to approach my future.

My understanding of faith is connected to action. D&C 58:27-28 teaches about being anxiously engaged in a good cause. For me, this means that I should actively seek opportunities to learn, improve, serve, and develop my abilities rather than waiting for opportunities to come to me.

Service is also central to how I view cybersecurity. Mosiah 2:17 teaches that serving others is a way of serving God. When I protect a person's information, help secure an organization's systems, or assist someone in understanding a security problem, I am using my skills to protect and serve others. This gives greater meaning to the technical work I am preparing to do.

Integrity is especially important because cybersecurity professionals may have access to highly sensitive information and powerful systems. My goal is to become someone who can be trusted to use that access responsibly. I want my decisions to reflect honesty, respect for privacy, accountability, and concern for the people affected by my work.

Reflection on My Spiritual Growth

One of the most meaningful experiences I included in this portfolio was my reflection on my temple covenants. Taking time away from schoolwork and technical responsibilities allowed me to slow down, pray, and think about where the Lord is leading me.

That experience helped me recognize more clearly that my spiritual life and professional life are not separate. Cybersecurity requires strong boundaries, careful authentication, monitoring, and protection of what is valuable. Similarly, spiritual growth requires me to establish boundaries, evaluate my choices, protect what is sacred, and continually work toward becoming more like Jesus Christ.

This reflection changed how I think about my future career. I do not want to leave my faith at the door when I enter the workplace. Instead, I want my faith to influence how I treat coworkers, how I handle confidential information, how I respond to difficult situations, and how I use my technical abilities.

Feedback and Improvement

The peer feedback I received also helped me improve my portfolio. One reviewer described the portfolio as clean and professional and specifically recognized the connection between my technical knowledge, personal values, and faith. They also said that my ethical dilemma analysis demonstrated thoughtfulness about integrity and responsibility.

The reviewer recommended adding a professional headshot to my About Me page and breaking up some of the longer paragraphs to improve readability. I took this feedback seriously because a professional portfolio should not only communicate what I have learned but should also present that information clearly to its audience.

I implemented the headshot recommendation on my About Me page and worked on improving the organization and readability of my content. This feedback helped me see my portfolio from the perspective of someone visiting it for the first time.

Looking Toward the Future

My future goals are now more intentional than they were when I began this portfolio. Professionally, I want to continue developing practical cybersecurity skills, pursue opportunities to strengthen my technical knowledge, and prepare myself for a career where I can protect systems and information.

I also want to continue developing the ability to communicate effectively, work with others, and make ethical decisions. Cybersecurity is constantly changing, so I know that I will need to continue learning throughout my career.

Spiritually, I want to continue strengthening my relationship with Jesus Christ through prayer, scripture study, temple worship, service, and personal reflection. I want to become more intentional about recognizing opportunities to serve others through both my personal life and my career.

My action plan reflects this commitment. I want to continue learning because cybersecurity constantly changes. I want to maintain integrity because technical ability without ethical responsibility can cause harm. I want to serve because my skills can protect people. Most importantly, I want to continue growing as a disciple of Jesus Christ while developing into a capable and trusted cybersecurity professional.

Conclusion

This portfolio has helped me see that my professional identity and spiritual identity do not need to compete with each other. They can strengthen each other. My education has given me technical skills, while my faith gives those skills direction and purpose.

I want to be the kind of cybersecurity professional who is technically capable, ethically responsible, willing to serve, and committed to lifelong learning. More importantly, I want my professional life to reflect the principles I believe in.

As I continue my education and enter the cybersecurity field, I hope to use the abilities I have developed to protect others, contribute positively to my community, and continue becoming the person the Lord wants me to be.

Reviewing my portfolio has helped me recognize how much I have grown throughout this program. I have expanded my technical knowledge in cybersecurity, Linux administration, networking, cloud computing, and security assessments while also strengthening my communication, teamwork, and ethical decision-making skills.

My portfolio now reflects both my professional accomplishments and my spiritual journey. It demonstrates how my faith influences my approach to cybersecurity and how integrity, service, and lifelong learning prepare me for a successful career protecting people and organizations.

Reflection on Agency

Doctrine & Covenants 58:26–28 teaches that we should be "anxiously engaged in a good cause" and use our agency to accomplish good things. This scripture has reminded me that my education and career are shaped by the choices I make each day. Success is not simply the result of completing assignments or passing classes, but of choosing to learn, improve, and serve others through the talents God has given me.

Throughout my cybersecurity program, I have chosen to challenge myself by learning new technologies, completing hands-on labs, and working collaboratively with classmates. These experiences have strengthened both my technical abilities and my confidence. I know that continuing to use my agency wisely will help me become a trustworthy cybersecurity professional who protects others with integrity.

Professional Growth

During my time at Ensign College, I have gained experience in cybersecurity, networking, Linux administration, AWS cloud computing, vulnerability assessments, and security policy development. Each course and project has strengthened my technical knowledge while teaching me the importance of teamwork, communication, and ethical decision-making.

I have also learned that cybersecurity is ultimately about protecting people. Technical skills are important, but they must be supported by integrity, accountability, and a commitment to lifelong learning. These experiences have prepared me to continue developing professionally while serving others through technology.

Personal Warning Signs

As I continue my education and prepare for a career in cybersecurity, I recognize several personal warning signs that could affect my growth. These include procrastination during busy weeks, becoming overwhelmed by multiple responsibilities, and focusing so much on technical tasks that I neglect personal or spiritual priorities.

To stay aligned with my values, I plan to maintain a regular schedule for scripture study, prayer, and personal reflection while also improving my time management. By keeping my spiritual and professional priorities balanced, I can continue growing both personally and professionally.

Spiritual Growth

My education has strengthened more than my technical abilities—it has also strengthened my faith. I have learned that spiritual principles such as faith, integrity, service, and continual learning apply directly to my future career. As I have studied both the gospel and cybersecurity, I have come to appreciate that protecting others' information is another way to serve my community.

This portfolio represents more than my academic accomplishments. It reflects my commitment to becoming a cybersecurity professional who is technically capable, ethically responsible, and guided by the teachings of Jesus Christ.

Christlike Attributes

Living Christlike Attributes

Throughout my education at Ensign College, I have learned that developing Christlike attributes is just as important as developing technical skills. Faith, integrity, service, patience, and humility have helped me overcome challenges while strengthening both my testimony and my professional abilities.

Integrity reminds me to always act honestly when handling sensitive information. Service motivates me to use my cybersecurity knowledge to protect others. Patience encourages me to continue learning even when technology becomes challenging, while humility reminds me that there is always more to learn. These attributes help me become a better disciple of Jesus Christ and prepare me to become an ethical cybersecurity professional.

Looking Forward

As I continue my education and begin my professional career, I hope to continue expanding my cybersecurity knowledge through certifications, hands-on experience, and lifelong learning. My goal is to contribute to organizations by improving security, reducing risk, and helping protect sensitive information.

I also hope to continue strengthening my faith, allowing my spiritual principles to guide my professional decisions. By combining technical excellence with integrity and service, I believe I can make a meaningful difference in both my career and my community.
